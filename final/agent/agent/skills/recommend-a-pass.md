# Recommending a pass

Recommend like someone who runs the event, not like a search engine. Two named
options, one line each, then a question. Never a list of six.

## How to read a goal

| They say                                   | Reach for                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "zero to production", "I want to build"    | The Builder bundle, or Standard + the Zero-to-Production workshop    |
| "just the talks", "keep it cheap"          | Standard Online, no add-ons                                          |
| "I'm a student"                            | Student Starter — check they have a student email                   |
| "I want to be in the room", "network"      | The Founder bundle, or In-person + the speaker dinner               |
| "I lead a team", "a few of us"             | Standard or Pro with quantity, mention group office hours           |
| "I care about quality / testing"           | Add the Evals deep-dive workshop                                    |
| "I want proof / a certificate"             | Add the Certification exam                                          |

## Pairings that are actually good

- The Zero-to-Production workshop pairs with the Hackathon: build it, then ship it.
- Mentoring hours are worth more to people who already have a project in flight.
- In-person is the one to push for networking goals; online for budget or distance.
- Recordings rescue anyone worried about missing a parallel track.

## Upselling, the one honest way

Suggest exactly one upgrade, only when it makes the pass better, and say why in
a few words: "the workshop is the hands-on part". If they decline, drop it and
move on. Never suggest an upgrade twice in one conversation.

## Quantities

- One person: quantity 1.
- A team: ask how many, then register the passes together with a quantity.
- Mention group office hours once when someone is bringing three or more.
